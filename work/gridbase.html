01PROBLEM

Exhibition floor planning is usually managed across two separate systems that don’t talk: a CAD or vector file for the spatial layout, and a spreadsheet for the exhibitor list. When a booth moves or an exhibitor cancels, both need to be updated manually. When neither is authoritative, nobody can trust either one.

02PROCESS

The design challenge was unifying those two representations without requiring users to think in two systems. The floor plan had to be the data — placing a booth on the canvas creates a record, not just a drawing. Assigning an exhibitor to a booth updates both the visual and the list simultaneously.

Conflict detection was a core requirement from the start: overlapping booths, double-assigned exhibitors, and booths that exceed their allocated square footage all need to surface immediately rather than at export time when it’s too late to fix cleanly.

03SOLUTION

A canvas-based floor planner where the spatial layout and the exhibitor database are a single model. Booth placement, sizing, and exhibitor assignment happen in one view. Conflicts highlight in real time. The list view and the floor plan are always the same data — there’s no sync step and nothing to reconcile.
